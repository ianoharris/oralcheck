# anatomy3d: layered head-and-neck model

`head.glb` is exported from the Z-Anatomy Blender atlas by `export_head.py`:
every skin region, bone, muscle, fascia, vessel, nerve, gland, lymph node,
eye and brain structure above the base of the neck, with vessels and nerves
converted from curves to tubes. The skin layers (epidermis, dermis,
hypodermis) are generated from the skin surface, because no atlas models them
as separate volumes; the epidermis is drawn about four times thicker than life
so it shows in a cut.

- "BodyParts3D", The Database Center for Life Science, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan,

`anatomy.bundle.js` bundles three.js r180 (MIT) with GLTFLoader, the meshopt
decoder, RoomEnvironment and OrbitControls.

## Rebuild

    blender -b --disable-autoexec --factory-startup Z-Anatomy/Startup.blend --python export_head.py -- head_raw.glb
    npx @gltf-transform/cli weld head_raw.glb w.glb && npx @gltf-transform/cli meshopt w.glb head.glb
    node build.mjs            # bundles src/main.js
    python render.py shots.json out/   # stills on the Mac GPU (add --soft for SwiftShader)

`head.part1.txt` / `head.part2.txt` are `head.glb` in base64, for the
published artifact (artifacts do not serve .glb). Regenerate them after a
re-export.

## textures/: the mouth's surfaces

`tongue_top`, `tongue_under`, `gums`, `lips` and `cheek` were generated by Ian
Harris with an image model (ChatGPT) on 22 Oct 2026 from prompts asking for
seamless macro textures of healthy tissue, then made tileable here; each
`*_h.jpg` is a relief map derived from its photo. The tonsils reuse `cheek`
with procedural crypts. They are illustrative surfaces, not photographs of a
real patient. Since 22 Oct the photos supply only fine variation and close-up
relief: the viewer divides each by its own average and takes its colour from
a palette matched by eye to clinical photographs Ian shared as references
(not shipped, not copied).

`tonsil`, `throat`, `skin`, and the second versions of `lips` and `cheek`
(replacing the ChatGPT ones, which had glare baked in) were generated by Ian with Gemini on 22 Oct 2026 from prompts
for flat-lit, seamless macro textures of healthy tissue, then evened out and
made tileable here (`tile.py`: divided by a heavy blur, then blended
with half-rolled copies one axis at a time). `throat` is used on the back wall
of the throat, its arches, the soft palate lining and the uvula; `skin` gives
the skin its pores and fine lines up close. Illustrative, not photographs of
a real patient.

## Generated mouth linings

The atlas has no lips, cheek lining, hard-palate lining or floor-of-mouth
lining. `export_head.py` builds them: the hard-palate lining from the underside
of the palate bones, and the rest as smooth surfaces swept along the dental
arch and measured, sector by sector, from the gums, skin and tongue around
them (cheeks, inner lips, lips, floor). They are fitted approximations, not
atlas anatomy. The cheek, inner-lip and floor linings are drawn from the
lining side only, so the open-mouth view works as a cutaway; the swept lips
are not shown (the lips are painted onto the skin in the Skin view instead).
The atlas's flat soft palate is given its real thickness.

Also generated, for the open-mouth "say ahh" view: the arches of the throat
(palatoglossal in front, palatopharyngeal behind), the back wall of the
throat, and a curved lining for the soft palate (the atlas's plate is hidden
in the Mouth view). The atlas's palatine tonsils are small and sit level with
the soft palate; they are moved down about 2 cm and enlarged to fill the niche
between the arches, where a tonsil sits in life. The skin layers are parted
along the line where the lips meet, so the face can open with the jaw; the
viewer moves the chin, lower lip and the skin over the jaw with the mandible
and lifts the upper lip. All of these are approximations shaped to the
surrounding atlas anatomy, not atlas anatomy.
