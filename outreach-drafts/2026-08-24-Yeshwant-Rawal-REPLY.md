Hi Dr. Rawal,

Thank you for reading it properly. I was expecting a polite brush-off, so a real critique with specifics in it is genuinely the most useful thing anyone has given me on this project.

Both of the concrete points are already live on the site.

The symptom question now includes growth of the gums and recent tooth mobility alongside the patches, non-healing ulcers, lumps, and dysphagia. That was a straightforward addition and I'd not have thought of either.

The 1 in 4 figure changed something more important, and I think you put your finger on the real weakness. The tool scores risk factors, so a person with none of them was being told "your current risk profile looks low", which invites exactly the wrong conclusion in the quarter of cases that have no conventional contributing factor. Any result in the lower tiers now carries a line saying that a low score does not rule anything out, that the score reflects risk factors rather than the mouth itself, and that anything persisting past two weeks should be examined regardless of what the tool said. You were right that the symptom question technically covers it, but it only covers it for someone who already knows to answer honestly about a symptom they've been ignoring, and the reassuring headline was working against that.

The systemic factors are the piece I haven't solved yet, and it's where I'd value your view most if you have the time. Fanconi anemia, post-transplant immunosuppression, and prior head and neck radiation are all substantially stronger signals than most of what I currently weight, but they're also rare enough that adding a question about each would cost every user time to catch very few people. My instinct is one question along the lines of "have you had an organ or bone marrow transplant, radiation to the head or neck, or a condition that suppresses your immune system?", weighted high, rather than three separate ones. I'd be interested in whether you think a single combined question is defensible clinically, or whether it flattens things that behave differently enough to matter.

Two asks, and please say no to either.

Rawal, BDS, MDS, MS, Marquette University School of Dentistry", with whatever wording and titles you'd want. To be clear about what I'd be claiming: that you read the methodology and gave feedback, which I've acted on. Not that you endorse the tool or vouch for its output.

Second, if you'd rather not attach your name to it, would you object to me quoting your line about it being done thoughtfully and based on evidence, unattributed or attributed only as an oral pathologist? Also completely fine if the answer is no to both.
