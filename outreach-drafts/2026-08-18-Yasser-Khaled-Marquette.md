Hi Dr. Khaled,

I'm a predental student at UW-Madison, and I built a free oral cancer risk screener called OralCheck (oralcheck.org). I'm trying to get it in front of people who teach oral medicine and diagnosis before it gets in front of many more patients, and I saw that oral cancer is among your research interests.

It's ten questions, about two minutes, written for the general public. Someone gets a risk tier, a plain explanation of which factors drove it, and a push toward a dental exam. It doesn't diagnose, and it's clear on the results page that the real screening is the exam itself.

Two specific things I'd value your opinion on, more than a general read:

(1) The symptom questions. The tool asks about a sore that hasn't healed in two weeks, red or white patches, a lump, and numbness or trouble swallowing, and it escalates the result when one is present regardless of the risk factor score. I think that's the right behavior, but I'm not confident the wording captures what a patient would actually notice, as opposed to what a clinician would look for.

(2) The results wording for the elevated tiers. I've tried to write something that gets someone to book an appointment without frightening them into avoiding it, and I'd imagine you've seen both failure modes in person far more than I have.

The full scoring methodology, with the odds ratios and sources behind each weight, is public at oralcheck.org/methods if you want to see how the number is built.

I'm not asking you to endorse it. I'm asking what you'd change, and I'd take a blunt answer gladly. If you'd rather point me to a resident or a student who'd find this a useful thing to pick apart, that would be just as helpful.

Thanks,
Ian Harris
