On monetization, there is none: no ads, no affiliate links, nothing sold, and no plan for any. If that ever changes I will treat it as a reason to go back through all of this before anything ships, not after.

On why I built it. I am predental, and the statistic that started this was that only about a quarter of oral cancers are found while they are still localized, where five-year relative survival is 88.7 percent, against 36.0 percent once it has spread to distant sites. That gap is not a treatment problem, it is a timing problem, and the exam that closes it is two minutes in a dental chair. I wanted something that would work on a phone, with no account and nothing stored, that could move somebody from not thinking about it to mentioning it at their next visit. [Ian: replace or extend this sentence with your actual reason if it is different.] It is in Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese now, which was not the original plan and happened because that is who turned out to be using it. My goal is not to build a diagnostic. It is to be a referral nudge that is honest about being only that, and if the right answer turns out to be that it should not exist in its current form, I would rather find that out from someone like you than not find it out.

On the methodology, taking your three points in order.

The oral cavity and oropharynx point is already the second entry in the limitations section on oralcheck.org/methods, and I agree it is a real weakness rather than a cosmetic one. HPV-16 drives the oropharyngeal side and tobacco and alcohol dominate the oral cavity side, and a single blended score serves neither cleanly. I have not solved it. The two honest options I can see are splitting the output into two scores, which roughly doubles the length and the explanation burden for a general-public tool, or restricting the scope to oral cavity only and dropping the HPV question, which removes the fastest-growing part of the problem. I do not know which is right, and it is a question I would genuinely like to be argued out of my current position on.

Your first point, that I am pooling odds ratios from studies in different populations and applying them to whoever opens the page, was not on that limitations page before your email and now is. It is the sharpest thing anyone has said about the tool. The tobacco weight comes from a meta-analysis of smoking cohorts, the betel weight from populations in South and Southeast Asia where use is common, the HPV weight from a US case-control study, and summing log-odds across them assumes those effect sizes transport to the person answering. That assumption is untested, and I now say on the page that it is the largest single source of uncertainty in the score, larger than any individual weight being somewhat off.

Your point about the cutoffs was fair and the page was worse than I thought. It documented how the four boundaries were anchored, which is not the same as saying where they came from, and a reader could easily have inferred more rigor than exists. It now states plainly that nothing derived them: they were set by judgment against reference profiles, sanity-checked so the tiers were not all but empty or all but full, and there is no sensitivity, specificity, or ROC curve behind them, because that requires outcome data the tool has never had.

Which brings me to the thing you said that I think is the most useful sentence anyone has sent me about this project, that a faculty mentor is the wrong person to tell me whether the stratification is valid and a biostatistician is the right one. I had been conflating those two roles, and I do not think I would have noticed on my own.

So the ask is smaller than the one I sent Dr. Glazer, and split in two.

The first is whether you or Dr. Davis would be willing to be the clinical half of that: not to validate anything, but to push on framing and scope, including on whether the oral cavity and oropharynx question above has an answer I am not seeing. Low frequency, and mostly me sending you something and you telling me it is wrong.

The second is a pointer rather than a commitment. Is there a route for an undergraduate to get a biostatistics consultation at UW, through ICTR or otherwise, and does that route require a faculty sponsor? If it does, that is a much smaller thing to ask of you than mentorship, and it might be the more useful one.

Either way, thank you for taking the time to actually look at it. The three criticisms were more useful than the answer to my original question would have been.
