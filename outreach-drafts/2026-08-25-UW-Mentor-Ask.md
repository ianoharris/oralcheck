Hi Dr. [Name],

I'm a junior at UW-Madison studying Biology with a Global Health minor, and I'm looking for a faculty mentor. I'd rather explain what for in one paragraph than ask for a meeting first.

I built a free oral cancer risk tool called OralCheck (oralcheck.org). It's ten questions, about two minutes, aimed at the general public. It returns a risk tier and a plain-language explanation of which of the person's own factors drove it, then points them toward a dental exam. It stores nothing: everything is computed in the browser, no answers are transmitted or retained. Each factor's weight is derived from the natural log of its published odds ratio, scaled so daily tobacco (OR 6.0, Gandini et al. 2008) maps to a weight of 8, with a separate interaction term for tobacco and alcohol. The full methodology and every source is public at oralcheck.org/methods.

Dr. Yeshwant Rawal at Marquette, who is President of the American Board of Oral and Maxillofacial Pathology, reviewed the methodology last week and has offered to be named as a reviewer. His department is now considering a waiting-room pilot. His first question to me was whether I have a faculty mentor at UW-Madison, and the honest answer is that I don't. That's the gap I'm trying to close, and I'd rather close it properly than let the project grow past what one undergraduate should be running alone.

What I'm asking for is not funding, lab space, or much of your time. It's someone willing to tell me when I'm wrong, and to be a name I can point to when a question like Dr. Rawal's comes up. If that turned into something more substantial later, a validation study or an abstract, I'd be glad, but I'm not asking for that now.

Two things I want to be upfront about. First, this is not a UW-Madison project and I have never presented it as one; the university has no affiliation with it, and I'm not looking to borrow its name. Second, I'm aware a risk questionnaire is a blunt instrument, that the literature I'm drawing on was not written to be turned into a consumer score, and that the USPSTF gives oral cancer screening an insufficient-evidence rating for primary care providers. Those are the weakest points and I'd want a mentor who pushes on them.

Would you be open to a short conversation, or if this isn't your area, is there someone you'd point me to?
